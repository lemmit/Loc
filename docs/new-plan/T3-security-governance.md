# T3 — Security, tenancy & governance

*Weak-spot #3: the enforcement machinery is genuinely strong (tenancy by construction, policy ladders, OIDC on all targets) but the DEFAULTS are demo-grade: authz default-open, finds ungatable, sessions shallow, concurrency opt-in. The governance spine (execution-context → tenancy → authorization) is mostly built — these missions finish and default-harden it.*

## M-T3.15 — The read surface: system reads, projection masking, and the find teardown — `plan` (awaiting sequencing sign-off) · **L** · P1
Grew out of M-T3.2 item 3: once the `requires` gate stopped living in the domain entity ([#2443](https://github.com/lemmit/Loc/pull/2443)), "so what secures the READS?" had no coherent answer. Four root causes, code-verified against `main` @ `1f917ac`. **(A) Loom has no construct for SYSTEM READS** — audit history borrows `find all`'s gate, workflow-instance reads (`/workflows/<wf>/instances`) are ungated AND invisible to `validateDefaultDeny`, and provenance is a write-only substrate with no read endpoint at all: three compiler-owned reads, three different answers. **(B) The two read surfaces cannot compose** — `loom.field-mask-projection-source` makes gated projections and `mask unless` mutually exclusive, and the diagnostic's own remedy points authors back at the *ungated* CRUD routes. **(C) The generated default is the unsecured surface** — the scaffold binds `.all`, not a projection. **(D) The find teardown has a hard prerequisite**: `ensureHistoryFind` copies `find all`'s `requires`, so deleting `requires` from `FindDecl` first makes `loom.audit-history-ungated` unsatisfiable — it would instruct authors to write syntax that no longer parses. Also recorded: `find all(): T[] requires <expr>` is enforced on node/.NET and **silently dropped on java/python/elixir** (`isAutoAllFind` never inspects `requires`), a documented workaround that is broken on three of five backends for as long as the syntax survives; and `commandHandler`/`queryHandler` routes have no header gate and are not enumerated by `validateDefaultDeny` (an `extern` handler has no body, so no gate surface at all). Two items carry the load — projection masking and the system-read construct; two are small, independent and security-relevant — gating workflow-instance reads and the handler gate.

## M-T3.16 — The lifecycle write gate: the canonical `create` / `destroy` body — `plan` · **M** · P1 (security)
The write-side twin of M-T3.15's read surface. A `requires` inside a canonical `create` / `destroy` is lowered into `canonicalCreate.statements` / `canonicalDestroy.statements` and read by **no backend** — so an authorization-gated create parses clean and leaves the route **OPEN**, and a `precondition` / `emit` / value-changing `assign` vanishes with it. [#2446](https://github.com/lemmit/Loc/pull/2446) closed the SILENCE (`loom.lifecycle-body-dropped`, with four exemptions that are the design) but not the gap; [#2487](https://github.com/lemmit/Loc/pull/2487) adds the guard's readable-surface contract (`create` → `currentUser` only; `destroy` → `currentUser` + `this`; neither → a parameter) as a CHECK, after the first emission attempt ([#2450](https://github.com/lemmit/Loc/pull/2450), withdrawn) assumed that contract and emitted an unbound receiver on all five backends. Two things gate the re-emission: an enforcement gate that can actually FAIL (#2450's passed four seeded mutations — polarity inversion on three backends, and .NET's destroy gate moved after the delete), and a decision on Elixir, whose scaffolded **LiveView calls the context directly**, around any controller-level gate. Also open and independent: a **value-object-typed field default emits non-compiling code** at the wire boundary (the domain class rendered into the wire DTO — `mypy --strict` and CS0246; TS compiles by structural typing alone), and an event-sourced create's guard renders into a domain `_init` that cannot reach a principal. [#2443](https://github.com/lemmit/Loc/pull/2443)'s `src/ir/util/op-gates.ts` and the route-builder unification (#2459–#2462) are the enablers: the DECLARATION half collapses to one edit in `api-surface.ts`, and the emission must reuse `op-gates.ts` rather than spell the same gate twice.

## M-T3.3 — P4 `deny` carve-outs — `done` · **M** · P2
Everything the checkpoint listed ships: grammar `deny [write] on <Aggregate>` (the `effect` discriminator on `PolicyReadRule`), `PolicyDenyIR` + lowering into `BoundedContextIR.policyDenies`, enrichment composing `buildDenyFilter` onto the read `contextFilters` (deny read) or `writeScopeFilter` (deny write) with deny winning over a widening allow, all three diagnostics (`loom.policy-deny-{unknown-aggregate,duplicate,shadows-allow}`), and the always-false fragment on all five backends — Drizzle contradiction / EF `false` / SQLAlchemy contradiction / JPQL `1 = 0` + `@SQLRestriction("1 = 0")` / Ecto `fragment("false")` — pinned by `test/ir/policy-deny.test.ts` + `test/generator/policy-deny.test.ts`. The open fork on `loom.policy-deny-shadows-allow` severity resolved as a warning (a deny that shadows an allow is legal and intentional; the diagnostic exists to surface a rule the author may not realise is dead).

**The residual, closed here: the feature had no fixture.** Until `test/fixtures/corpus/policy-deny.ddd` no `.ddd` anywhere — corpus, `examples/`, or `web/src/examples/` — carried `deny`, so it was covered by unit + generator tests and never by a build: it never reached the five-backend corpus compile matrix and its emitted schema never reached `schema-load`. A fragment can render exactly right and still not compile in place (the Elixir deny-write command load must underscore its now-principal-free `_current_user` parameter or `--warnings-as-errors` rejects the module). The fixture declares both seams, a `crossTenant` aggregate denied with NO tenant filter (so the always-false term stands alone — the shape most likely to strand an unused parameter or import), a named `find` on the read-denied aggregate (the deny term must be AND-ed into the author's own `where`, not just the auto findAll), and an undenied control — so a backend that renders the term into the wrong query site, or into every site, shows up as a diff rather than as uniform silence. Registered in `E2E_LESS_CORPUS_FIXTURES`: `deny` now compiles everywhere but still has no runtime caller, which is M-T9.13's drain, not this mission's.

Verified on all five (`tsc --noEmit` / `ruff` + `mypy --strict` / `gradle testClasses bootJar` / `dotnet build /warnaserror` / `mix compile --warnings-as-errors`) plus `schema-load`; mutation-proven that the fixture reaches those gates (removing it drops schema-load 41 → 40 and reds `corpus-coverage`). The fixture earned its keep immediately: it exposed an unrelated python emitter bug — a column named `text` matched the schema emitter's `\btext\b` import scan, adding an import nothing invoked and failing `ruff` F401 for any model with such a field — fixed in the same PR.
One consequence is tracked elsewhere: the fixture immediately exposed that `policy { deny }` **crashes codegen on `persistence: dapper`** (and `deny write` is unenforceable there) — that is [M-T6.31](T6-backend-parity.md), and it raised the `DAPPER_COMPILE_SKIP` ratchet 2 → 3. A `done` here means "ships on all five backends' PRIMARY adapters", not everywhere. Also: `policy-deny` sits in `E2E_LESS_CORPUS_FIXTURES` — the deny behaviour ("a denied read 404s / lists empty over HTTP") compiles everywhere and is runtime-proven nowhere; that drain is M-T9.13's.

## M-T3.7 — Tenancy hardening tail — `partial` · **M** · P2
(a) **DONE** — see below; (b) **DONE** (verified 2026-08-05) — the runtime matrix already reaches elixir: `test:tenancy-elixir` / `test:tenancy-hierarchy-elixir` ship and `tenancy-e2e.yml` is a 10-cell matrix (all five backends × {flat, hierarchy}); (c) **DONE** — see below; (d) `loom.tenancy-claim-type-mismatch` **already ships** (`tenancy-checks.ts`), alongside `loom.tenant-owned-claim-type`; **(e) claim-TYPED capability fields** — the remaining half of (a), see below.

**(c) Landed — a malformed tenant claim reads empty instead of 500-ing.** Under `tenancy by user.<claim> of <Registry>` with a `guid` registry id and a `string` claim (`tenancyClaimBinding === "guid-from-string"`, the common JWT shape), the derived self-scope `this.id == currentUser.<claim>` bound raw token text straight against the id. An authenticated token carrying `tenantId: "not-a-guid"` therefore died in the uuid parser — `IllegalArgumentException` out of Hibernate query preparation, `Ecto.Query.CastError`, pg `invalid input syntax for type uuid`, SQLAlchemy `ValueError` — and answered an ordinary bad token with a 500 and a stack trace. It always failed CLOSED (no leak, no cross-tenant read), so this was about the response, not the isolation.

**.NET was already right** and is what the other four were ported to: `Guid.TryParse(…) ? new <Agg>Id(g) : null`, i.e. coerce in the host language and let a NULL match no row. Same shape as (a) with the roles reversed — there Java was the correct one and four backends needed porting. Each backend renders its own native fail-closed form (a UUID-shape ternary falling to Drizzle's always-false term; `Ecto.UUID.dump/1` → nil, compared through a `fragment` so the PK index still serves the read; `User.guid_claim` → `None` → `IS NULL`; a `<claim>AsUuid()` accessor on the Java principal → `= NULL`), all gated on one shared IR predicate, `guidFromStringSelfScope` — the single shape whose principal side can fail to parse.

Java's Criteria path is fixed in the same change: a `criterion` on the registry emits a `tenantScope(User)` Specification that bypasses the `@Query` reads, and it compared the `@EmbeddedId` *embeddable* to a raw `String` — broken for every claim, well-formed or not. It now navigates to `id.value` and binds through the same accessor.

Why it hid: the shape was in `tenancy-owned.ddd` all along; only the malformed VALUE was missing, because every claim the isolation harness sends is a real org id. The fix therefore lands the probe in `assertCrossTenantIsolation`, so all ten `tenancy-e2e` cells assert the STATUS (200 + `[]`, 404 by id) — asserting emptiness alone would pass on a 500, since the read already failed closed. Compile-verified on all five backends; per-backend generator suites pin that no read path binds the raw claim.

**(e) The claim's TYPE is still pinned, now that its NAME is not.** `tenantOwned` provides `tenantId: string` as a literal, so a system declaring `tenancy by user.orgId` where `orgId: guid` is rejected by `loom.tenant-owned-claim-type` rather than supported — the capability's column would have to be typed from the declaration the same way (a) taught its filter and stamp to bind from it. The workaround the diagnostic suggests (declare the claim `string`; guid values round-trip as text) is real and costs nothing today, which is why this is P3 rather than a defect: nothing is silently wrong, and the gate is honest. It becomes worth doing when a capability needs a non-string discriminator, or when capability fields gain declaration-dependent types generally. Note the asymmetry that makes it more than a rename: the registry self-scope already handles a guid claim (it compares against the registry's own guid id via `tenancyClaimBinding`), so today one half of tenancy accepts a guid claim and the other refuses it.

**(a) Landed — the declared claim now binds the `tenantOwned` stamp and filter.** The capability hardcoded `currentUser.tenantId` on the principal side of both, so `tenancy by user.<claim>` only ever worked for one claim name: a non-`tenantId` claim emitted a backend referencing a claim the principal does not have — a compile error on node/.NET/Java, a per-request 500 on Python/Elixir. It crashed rather than failing open (no cross-tenant leak), but the documented surface was unusable for every other name.

Two things kept it alive. `prelude.ts` carried a note claiming the tenancy validators verified the declared claim *is* `tenantId` — they never did; they check the claim EXISTS and that its TYPE binds, never its name. And all three tenancy fixtures declared the claim as `tenantId`, the one name that makes the hardcode accidentally correct, so the corpus compile tier, the 10-cell `tenancy-e2e` matrix and the behavioral tier were structurally incapable of observing it.

Fixed in **phase ⑥, not in the macro**: a capability is a prelude definition built at language-module init with no system in scope, so it *cannot* read the declaration — enrichment is where per-system facts get bound, and it already derives the registry self-scope from the same `sys.tenancy`. That asymmetry is what made the bug legible: both halves appeared in one generated project disagreeing, the registry reading `.orgId` while the tenant-owned aggregate read `.tenantId`. The rewrite is scoped to capability-derived nodes, so an author's own `currentUser.tenantId` survives (a principal may legitimately carry an unrelated field of that name). The ROW column stays `tenantId` everywhere — the capability owns it. Four backends also reached for the column constant on the principal side of their scope-sentinel renderer and now read it off the sentinel (`deepScopeTenantClaim`, twin of `deepScopeAnchorClaim`); Java already did this correctly.

Gated by `test/fixtures/corpus/tenancy-claim-name.ddd` — the first fixture whose claim is deliberately not `tenantId` — which joins all five compile tiers and the behavioral tier, with a wire golden minted from node. Compile-verified on all five backends and runtime-verified on node/PGlite (create → filtered read returns exactly the created row; a stamp/filter disagreement returns 0).

## M-T3.9 — Audit promotion: `audited` aggregate-wide + `logged` — `partial` · **M** · P2
Boolean `audited` ships on all five, ops AND lifecycle (verified 2026-07-13: `AUDIT_OP_BACKENDS` = `AUDIT_LIFECYCLE_BACKENDS` = all 5, `system-checks.ts:2464-2465`; the old plan's "gate claims node support that ships nowhere" note was stale).

**Landed — the aggregate-wide form `aggregate X audited { … }`.** Sits in the header region beside `crossTenant` (same category: per-aggregate participation in a compiler-owned facility — see the M-T5.17 sort-by-meaning amendment). Covers every **public** command action; `private` operations are never audited, which is the opt-out for an internal/high-churn command. **Resolved during lowering** into the per-command flags rather than threaded through emitters — `op.audited` is read at ~30 sites across the five backends with no chokepoint, so resolving early means every existing gate keeps working and a site that forgot to consult an aggregate-level flag cannot silently drop coverage. No new `AggregateIR` field. Verified generate-and-inspect on all five backends with zero emitter edits; corpus fixture `audited.ddd` puts it through the per-backend COMPILE tier. Validator warns when `audited` is declared on an aggregate with no public command action (an empty trail reads as authoritative while lying by omission). Also removed dead code: `auditedOpsOf`/`aggHasAuditedOp` were exported from the .NET/Java/Python audit emitters, called by nothing, and had drifted apart.

**The argument form `audited(actions|access|events|off)` is DROPPED — do not build it.** Each of its five options dissolves on inspection:
- `off` → unnecessary; `private` is the opt-out, and the aggregate-wide/per-command forms are alternatives rather than layered, so there is no default to negate.
- `actions` → it *is* the shipped default; `audited(actions)` ≡ `audited`.
- `access` (read-auditing) → **a different feature**: different table, different trigger point (query pipeline, not command boundary), different volume profile (writes on every read), different consumer. Needs its own mission and its own modifier on views/finds — `access` on an *operation* is meaningless, yet the paren form would admit it.
- `events` → **subsumed by `persistedAs: eventLog`**, which already gives a richer, replayable history. A second, weaker event-emission mechanism competing with real event sourcing.
- `containsPii` → **derivable** from the existing `sensitive(pii, …)` field tags; don't restate at the audit site what the field already declares.

Dropping it also resolves a head-on collision with **M-T5.17**, which removed the paren modifier form and codemodded the corpus for it — `audited(actions)` would have reintroduced exactly that syntax. `audited` therefore stays a boolean permanently.

**Remaining:** the `logged` marker (unrelated to audit; keep), and the AuditRecord snapshot enrichment. The read side — exposing `audit_records` as an entity-history view — was deliberately not part of the original mission; it is tracked in its own section below.

**The read path — shipped on all five.** `audit_records` had no read endpoint on any backend, so the write substrate existed with nothing consuming it. (`auditTableShape` into `MigrationsIR` — the prerequisite this note used to list — landed separately in #2325, and #2379 did the same for `provenance_records`; both are ordinary companion tables now.) Landed here: the platform-neutral `AuditEntry` wire shape + diff boundary (`src/ir/util/audit-history.ts`), a derived `find history(id)` injected in the auto-`findAll` enrichment slot (as `RepositoryIR.historyFind` — *beside* `finds`, not in it: `finds` has ~120 generic consumers that all assume a find reads the aggregate's own table, and a missed skip-guard would emit a broken route rather than fail), and `GET /<agg>/{id}/history` on **all five backends** (#2378 node + python, #2404 java, #2406 .NET, #2408 elixir), with the wire golden (`test/behavioral/wire-golden/audit-history.json`) minted from node as the answer key the rest diff against — each of the other four matched it with 0 divergences on a real boot. Because A≡golden ∧ B≡golden ⇒ A≡B, the `audit-history` corpus row reading `ALL` is a *proven* cross-backend equality, not five self-assertions.

Two ports carried backend-local wrinkles worth knowing. **Phoenix:** `at` is written through Ecto's `:utc_datetime` (second precision), so ordering by `at` alone ties for two commands inside one second — the query adds an `audit_id` (UUIDv7, time-ordered) tiebreak, which no other backend needs. **.NET:** the snapshot columns were the only place a backend diverged on how the shared `jsonb` DDL was *bound* — a serialized string rather than an object — so #2406 moved them to `System.Text.Json.Nodes.JsonNode?` and `docs/audit.md` now states the binding for all five as one rule (a porter indexes the snapshot; nobody parses). That change collided productively with #2412, which found that `mask unless` + `audited` never compiled on .NET at all (CS0128 — a fixed `__maskUser` pattern-variable name, declared twice in one handler body).

**Remaining:** the frontend half — the `Timeline` walker primitive (#2400) landed, and the API-client `history()` + scaffolded History section (#2407, gated by #2411) complete it. The scaffolded section is narrower than the primitive: it ships on the four JS-family frontends, because Feliz maps non-`byId` reads to `All<Plural>`, Flutter skips them in `collectFlutterReads`, and HEEx assigns the list read rather than the trail. Giving the clients a `history()` method is the explicit bill for putting `historyFind` beside `finds` — they iterate `finds`.

The authorization slice is done and is the load-bearing one — `before`/`after` store raw field values *unmasked* **on four of five backends** (the write happens server-side inside the transaction, while `mask unless` is per-caller) — **.NET diverges (open item, promoted from a prose-only note 2026-08-05):** its audited command handlers project snapshots through the *masked* serializer, so a masked field is recorded as `null` whenever the acting principal fails the predicate — the stored trail depends on *who* performed the write. `docs/auth.md` §"Known divergence — .NET audit snapshots" says "tracked as a follow-up" but nothing tracked it until now; it is a runtime-value divergence with no wire waiver, and this mission owns it. Beyond that, so an endpoint that skips the masking pass publishes every masked field's full change history. Three guards, each re-established explicitly: the read gate is **copied from the aggregate's `find all`** (ungated + `denyByDefault` → `loom.audit-history-ungated`); capability filters ride **entity reachability** via `findById` rather than a predicate on `audit_records` (which carries no tenant column), so a cross-tenant history 404s exactly as the entity read does; and a masked field's `FieldChange` is **dropped, not redacted** — "admin changed `salary`" is itself the leak. Field diffs are derived at read time from the two snapshots and exclude managed/stamp *and* token fields (`after` is captured post-save, so `updatedAt`/`updatedBy`/`version` differ on every entry). Only *successful* commands are recorded (`status` hardcoded `"ok"`, insert rides the transaction), so this answers "what changed", not "who tried" — stated in [`docs/audit.md`](../audit.md) rather than left to be discovered.

Fixed in passing, both pre-existing: **Python's `mask unless` never redacted a multi-word field** (`to_wire` writes the wire key verbatim, but `to_wire_masked` redacted `d[snake(name)]`, so a camelCase field kept its full value — every mask fixture used `salary`, one word, where the two spellings coincide), and **collection ops in a `test e2e` body were emitted verbatim** (`trail.first()` → `trail.first()`, which JS arrays do not have, so the suite died with a TypeError at the assertion rather than failing to compile).

## M-T3.16 — Lifecycle `create`/`destroy` guard: from silently-dropped to enforced — `open` (design decisions pending) · **M–L** · **P0 — the top open security gap** ⭐
*(Minted 2026-08-10. The whole PR cluster was mission-less; this entry is the coordination point.)* **The bug, live on `main` today:** a `requires` (or `precondition`) written in a STATE aggregate's canonical `create`/`destroy` body is lowered into `canonicalCreate.statements` / `canonicalDestroy.statements` — and **no emitter on any of the five backends reads either**. The guard silently does not enforce; **the route is left open**; the 403 is not even declared (`errorStatuses("create"|"destroy", false, …)` hardcoded at `api-surface.ts:439,485`). A literal default in a create body (`status := 0`) is likewise silently promoted to a required client field. Latent because the only repo idiom is `field := <same-named param>` (which the field-derived input reproduces by coincidence), event-sourced creates DO render their body, and workflow creates do too — so `showcase.ddd`'s two guarded creates are unaffected and #2446's 331-file scan found 0 firing.
**State of the cluster (2026-08-10):** #2443 (hoist the aggregate-OPERATION gate out of the entity into the handler — the adjacent Layer B, self-contained, creates `src/ir/util/op-gates.ts`, Phoenix already does it right on the context) lands **first**; then #2446 (the honest `loom.lifecycle-body-dropped` error + a real Java lowering fix); then #2487 (the readable-surface CONTRACT, `loom.lifecycle-guard-unreadable` — create reads `currentUser` only, destroy adds `this`; the check whose absence sank the last attempt); then a **new re-port PR** for the emission — **#2450 is closed as a vehicle** (its "mutation-proven" claim was retracted by its own adversarial review: four seeded polarity/ordering defects passed 10/10; its base is a stale intermediate commit; the route-builder unification conflicts in all ten emitter files). Its three review comments are the design record for the re-port; its surviving assets (semantics, `lifecycleGuards` incl. the ES double-gate exclusion, `corpus/lifecycle-guard.ddd`, `docs/auth.md` §) port forward.
**Open design decisions (all pending, blocking the re-port):** (1) the Elixir gate must live in the CONTEXT, not the REST controller — the scaffolded LiveView calls `create_<agg>(params)` directly and `destroy_<agg>!/1` is a second documented front door (probably = reuse `op-gates.ts`/`collectOpGuardClauses`); (2) event-sourced guarded create — the guard renders into `_init` where `currentUser` is a free identifier (doesn't compile): route-gate, thread a principal, or reject; (3) guarded lifecycle on a deployable without `auth:` references an auth module never generated (pre-existing class — finds/operations too; needs a validator); (4) how much the contract rejects (params shadowing fields — #2450's finding 9 says the blanket param ban is over-broad for the dominant idiom); (5) status ordering for guarded-create-with-invalid-body (403 vs 422, no golden); (6) any new enforcement gate must assert **polarity** and deny-**before-delete** — the two probes whose absence let four seeded defects pass.
